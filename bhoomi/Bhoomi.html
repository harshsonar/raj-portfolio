Each block is one kanal. In much of Punjab, eight of them make an acre.

Children rarely meet these units in class, so we made a game where they build a plot, grow crops on it and discover how much land they have made.

Experiential learning

Understanding through touch and visual feedback.

We wanted cultural knowledge that children can touch, explore and understand.

A game built around the land itself.

Bhoomi is a tangible user interface game that connects physical pieces with a digital farm.

Children explore a region, find its traditional land unit, lay out land tiles and grow crops to feel the scale of the land.

The game brings together physical play, game based learning and local storytelling.

Image: the board with tiles, regions and crops, with callouts

Test the experience before building the system.

A working tangible interface would have taken a lot of technical effort. So we used the Wizard of Oz method: the interaction was simulated by hand, which let us spend our time on ideas, interaction design and testing with users.

Making cultural knowledge experiential.

Bhoomi looks at how tangible interaction can make India's traditional land measurement systems easier for young learners to reach.

Physical handling, digital storytelling and game mechanics turn an abstract unit into something children can build, see and relate to.

Interaction design can connect cultural knowledge with a sense of space.
