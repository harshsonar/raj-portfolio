01 · Background

Presenting situation

Client A was diagnosed with type 2 diabetes four years ago and takes metformin twice daily. At intake he reported persistent tiredness in the afternoons, frequent thirst, and a work schedule that often meant skipping breakfast and eating a large, late dinner.

His usual day included sweetened tea three times a day, white rice at most meals, and little fruit or vegetables outside of dinner. He had tried to cut sugar before but stopped after a few weeks because meals felt “bland”.

Average intake across the three recalls came to about 2,450 kcal per day. Carbohydrate supplied roughly 58% of energy, mostly from white rice, refined-flour bread, and sweetened drinks. Protein was about 0.8 g per kg of body weight, and vegetables appeared on only one meal in five.

03 · Nutrition diagnosis

Problem, cause, and evidence

The diagnosis follows the PES format: problem, etiology, and signs or symptoms.

Problem
Excessive intake of refined carbohydrate and sugar, with low dietary fiber.
Related to
Irregular meal timing, limited knowledge of portion sizes and carbohydrate quality, and habitual use of sweetened drinks.
As evidenced by
Fasting glucose of 9.1 mmol/L, HbA1c of 8.4%, fiber intake of 11 g/day, and three sweetened drinks per day.

Regular meal pattern

Three meals and one planned snack, spaced four to five hours apart. Breakfast is kept small but not skipped.

Carbohydrate quality

Half the plate is non-starchy vegetables. Whole-wheat roti or millet replaces refined bread, and rice is served in a measured portion.

Beverages

Unsweetened tea or water replaces sweetened drinks. Sugar was reduced over two weeks rather than stopped at once.

Activity pairing

A 20-minute walk after the largest meal, most days of the week, agreed with the physician and adjusted for knee comfort.

A sample day

  1. 08:00Steel-cut oats cooked in milk, with walnuts and a boiled egg.
  2. 11:00A pear with a small handful of peanuts.
  3. 13:30Two whole-wheat rotis, dal, a vegetable sabzi, cucumber salad, and plain curd.
  4. 16:30Roasted chana and unsweetened tea.
  5. 20:00Grilled fish or paneer with mixed vegetables and one small portion of brown rice, followed by a 20-minute walk.

Adherence was strongest in the first six weeks, when the meal pattern was the main focus. A work trip in week 7 disrupted meals for about ten days. The team responded with a portable snack plan, which the client used for the rest of the program. Fiber rose steadily from 11 to 26 g per day, and sweetened drinks were fully removed by week 4.

The physician made no change to metformin during the program. Diet changes can lower glucose, so the team checked for early symptoms of low blood sugar at each review.

06 · Reflection

What this case suggests

Start with structure, not a menu. Fixing the meal pattern was the earliest win. Once breakfast was back in the day, portion decisions became easier.

Swap rather than subtract. Replacing refined flour with whole grain kept the meals familiar, which made the change last longer than the earlier attempt to cut sugar.

Use fiber as a proxy. A daily fiber target gave the client a concrete number to track, and it moved with carbohydrate quality without requiring weighed food records.

Plan for disruption. The week 7 trip showed the value of a portable plan built before the disruption, not after it.

This is an illustrative demo. The client, values, and results are invented to show how a case is structured. It is not clinical guidance. A registered dietitian and the treating clinician should individualize any real plan.
